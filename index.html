Objective and Scope

Robot soccer is a uniquely demanding benchmark for humanoid robotics: a robot must perceive and localize while moving, maintain balance under contact and uncertainty, execute agile whole-body skills, recover from falls, reason over long horizons, communicate with teammates, and make decisions in a fast, partially observable, adversarial environment. This workshop connects the RoboCup community with the broader humanoid robotics, robot learning, control, and embodied-AI communities to examine how these capabilities can be advanced jointly rather than as isolated modules.

The scope spans hardware-aware locomotion and manipulation, perception and state estimation, sim-to-real learning, foundation and vision-language-action models, multi-robot coordination, safety, benchmarking, and reproducibility. Particular attention is given to methods that transfer beyond soccer to human-centered applications in warehouses, healthcare, inspection, entertainment, and everyday environments.

The program combines invited talks, peer-reviewed extended abstracts, short oral presentations, a panel, and an interactive roadmap session. Subject to venue and safety approval, teams are invited to bring robots for live demonstrations or recorded demo presentations. The intended outcome is a shared set of research priorities, benchmark needs, and open challenges supporting measurable progress toward the RoboCup 2050 goal and robust general-purpose humanoids.

Expected Attendance: ~50 participants from academia and industry

Submission & List of Topics

The workshop is open to a broad audience from academia and industry researching on Autonomous humanoid soccer players, Humanoid control for robot soccer, Foundational models for humanoid soccer robots, Multi-agent communication, and Humanoid robot soccer as a benchmark for embodied intelligence.

Autonomous Humanoid Soccer Visual perception, localization, game understanding
Humanoid Control Dynamic locomotion, whole-body control, physical interaction
Foundation Models Vision-language-action models, multimodal learning
Multi-Agent System Communication, coordination, semantic representations
Benchmarking Reproducible evaluation, shared datasets, sim-to-real transfer
Robot Safety Real-world deployment, robust execution

We invite authors to submit scientific papers ranging from 2 to 6 pages, with additional space for references and appendices. We accept different types of works, including preliminary findings, case studies, position papers, surveys and cutting-edge research on the workshop topics. Accepted papers are presented in short oral presentations.

Authors should submit their papers formatted according to the IEEE two-column format, which is also used for contributions to the main conference. Use the following templates to create the paper and generate or export a PDF file: LaTeX or MS-Word.

Authors need to submit their PDF via OpenReview. Each paper receives at least two reviews. All papers are reviewed using a single-blind review process: authors declare their names and affiliations in the manuscript for the reviewers to see, but reviewers do not know each other's identities, nor do the authors receive information about who has reviewed their manuscript.
